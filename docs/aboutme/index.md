What does it mean to defend an engineering decision : and do you currently know how to do it?

Defending an engineering decision means that you can extensively justify the method of solving the issue. The proposed solution must not only be proven to work in theory through calculations but also have pros outweigh cons when comparing it to alternative solutions. There are many fronts a decision can win through and each project/company will weigh things differently. When designing a toy that only sells with a tiny profit margin to casual buyers, the engineers are going to be making decisions based off of price, safety, intuitive design, and bulk manufacturing. Compare that to a company making turbines who attracts large clients through having a better performing or more reliable product, the engineers are going to be thinking a lot more about reliability and lifespan of the turbine, power output, efficiency, ease of installation, etc. With how many potential issues there are in real life application, I would say I can currently research and defend a decision as I stumble upon problems but I am always trying to improve upon my ability to see problems quickly. The point of prototyping is to find these issues but the more I'm able to catch in the concept stage, the cheaper and quicker the results will follow.
